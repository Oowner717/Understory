Queen of Hawthorn · Queen of Wands   [wands-queen]
keywords  warmth as craft, hosting, the room people choose
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. Everyone ends up in the kitchen. Not the room with the good chairs. A bloom
  above one sprig. Warmth like yours is a made thing. Kept up over years.
  People read it as a temperament. You built the room they choose.
    Q. Who ends up in your kitchen?

 2. Six petals open above a budded branch. You did the work of being easy to be
  around this week. None of it looked like work. Hospitality is a craft. Its
  tools never show. That is why it gets taken for a mood.
    Q. What did that cost you this week?

 3. You keep the good chairs in the other room. Everyone sits in here anyway. A
  bloom above one sprig. Warmth done well looks like luck. Yours took a
  decade. The people warming their hands at it have no idea it was built.
    Q. Where did you learn to do that?

REVERSED

 1. The kitchen filled again on Saturday and you enjoyed it. Genuinely. Being
  the warm one costs something only when it runs one way. This week it did
  not. One of them washed up without being asked. That is the whole report.
    Q. Who returned it this week?

 2. The pot is on and you are tired of the pot. Hosting has become the thing you
  are for. Warmth that cannot take a night off has turned into a job
  description. You never announced it. The guests are the last to know.
    Q. When was your last night off?

 3. A room can be worked. You know how. Warmth on demand is a real skill and a
  strange thing to own. The difference between hosting and charm is whether
  you like anyone in the kitchen.
    Q. Hosting, or performing?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Everyone ends up in the kitchen. There are better chairs two rooms away and
  they stay empty, and the reason is not the room. It is whoever is standing
  in it. That person has been standing in it for years, getting good at
  something that does not look like a skill from outside. Warmth of this grade
  is cultivated. It gets decided on at some point and then maintained, and the
  maintenance is mostly remembering things about people and asking the second
  question. Hawthorn flowers hard and early and gets called a weed for the
  trouble. What goes unsaid is that this is work, unpaid and largely
  unremarked. The people warming their hands at it read it as a temperament
  rather than a practice, which is the compliment and the problem in one.
  Yours has a start date. You could probably name the year.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three states of the warm room. In the first it runs both ways. The kitchen
  fills, you enjoy it, and one of them washes up without being asked, and that
  single act is the whole difference between hosting and service. In the
  second the warmth has become a job. It cannot take a night off, because the
  role was assigned by repetition rather than agreement, and the guests are
  always the last to know the host is tired. That state answers to one
  cancelled Saturday and very little else. In the third the technique has
  outrun the affection. Warmth on demand is a real accomplishment and a
  strange thing to own, because a room can be worked by someone who does not
  much like the people in it, and from inside the room it feels identical.
  Only the host can tell those apart.

Queen of Bellflower · Queen of Cups   [cups-queen]
keywords  level water, holding others, an unsounded floor
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. Four people called you first this month. A bloom above one hanging bell. You
  hold level. They talk until they find out what they think. That is a
  service. Your own floor has never been sounded.
    Q. Who calls you first?

 2. A full open bloom above a hanging bellflower. Depth like this is a service
  other people use without measuring it. You held steady on a phone call. The
  other person worked out what they felt. They remember the room as calm.
    Q. What did you hold steady through?

 3. You are good at this and it is not free. A bloom above a bell. Being the
  level one means the other person leaves lighter than they came. Every time.
  Where that weight goes is a question no one thinks to ask.
    Q. Where does the weight go?

REVERSED

 1. You held level and it cost you almost nothing. That call went both ways.
  Depth is only a burden when it runs one way. One of them asked you a real
  question back on Thursday. Rare enough to write down.
    Q. Who asked you something back?

 2. The water is not level this week. You are still taking calls. Holding steady
  is a skill that hides its own limit. A bloom above a bell. No gauge anywhere
  on the plate. The floor is further down than the callers think.
    Q. When were you last the one calling?

 3. Everyone brings you theirs and none of them has yours. That arrangement is
  comfortable. You built it. Being the deep one is one way of staying
  unsounded. The bell hangs and does not ring for itself.
    Q. Who has yours?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Everyone calls you first. That is a fact about your month rather than a
  compliment, and it has a plain explanation. You hold level while the other
  person finds out what they think. The water does not move. They talk, the
  surface stays flat, they leave lighter than they arrived, and the whole
  exchange gets remembered by them as a calm room rather than as a thing you
  did. Bellflower hangs and holds its shape in wind. Depth of this kind is
  genuinely useful and genuinely load-bearing for the people who lean on it.
  What goes unmeasured is the floor. No one sounds it, partly because the
  surface gives no reading and partly because being the deep one is a workable
  way of never being asked. The callers are not withholding. They have simply
  never been given the impression there was anything to ask about.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Deep water runs three ways. In the first it runs in both directions. A call
  happens, you hold level, and at some point the other person asks you a real
  question and waits for the answer, and that single reversal is what
  separates a friendship from a service. In the second the water is not level
  and the calls keep coming. Holding steady is a skill that conceals its own
  limit, and the people who rely on it have no gauge and no reason to suspect
  one is wanted. That state ends when the level one says so, and rarely
  otherwise. In the third the depth has become the arrangement. Everyone
  brings theirs, none of them has yours, and this is comfortable, because
  never being sounded is safer than being sounded and found ordinary. The bell
  hangs and does not ring for itself.

Queen of Gladiolus · Queen of Swords   [swords-queen]
keywords  candor once, kindness with an edge, restraint
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You said the true thing once and then passed the potatoes. Once is the whole
  technique. Candor that repeats itself has stopped being candor and started
  being pressure. Everyone at the table hears the difference.
    Q. What did you say once?

 2. A bloom above one blade leaf, both plainly drawn. The truest sentence in the
  room got said at your end of the table. Then the subject moved on. No one
  had to answer it. That restraint is the rare part, not the honesty.
    Q. Who did not have to answer?

 3. Six petals open above a narrow blade. Kindness and accuracy are not in
  tension here. That is what makes this the hardest card in the suit to
  imitate. You managed both this week, in one sentence. Then you let it go.
    Q. Where did you stop?

REVERSED

 1. You said it once, kindly, and it landed. One serving. The person went home
  and thought about it. That only happens when a thing gets said a single
  time. Second helpings of a true sentence get eaten as an argument.
    Q. Whose evening did that improve?

 2. You said the true thing three times. By the third, a weapon. Accuracy is not
  a permit. The bloom sits above the blade and the blade is still a blade. The
  difference between candor and cruelty is often just the count.
    Q. How many times did you say it?

 3. The potatoes went round and the true thing stayed unsaid. Not from
  cowardice, from your own fair estimate of the cost. The estimate was
  probably right. Kindness that never risks anything is manners. Manners are
  useful and they are a different instrument.
    Q. What stayed unsaid at the table?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The true thing gets said once, kindly, at a table where the potatoes are
  still going round. Then the subject changes. That is the entire method, and
  hardly anyone can do the second half. Saying the accurate thing is not rare,
  since most families contain at least one person willing to. Saying it a
  single time and leaving the room intact is rare, because being right is
  exciting and the temptation is to press. Pressed twice, a true sentence
  becomes an argument, and the person it was aimed at spends the evening
  defending rather than thinking. Gladiolus cuts clean when it cuts once.
  Yours cut once this week, at a table, and then the potatoes went round
  again. What gets measured here is not honesty. It is the restraint bolted
  onto the honesty, and the restraint is the part that took thirty years to
  fit.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three states, and the middle one is the common failure. In the first the
  sentence gets said once, lands, and gets thought about later at home, which
  is the only place a true sentence ever does its work. In the second the
  sentence gets said three times. By the third repetition it has become a
  weapon regardless of how accurate it is, because being right is not a
  permit, and the person on the other end has stopped considering it and
  started defending. Candor and cruelty are frequently separated by the count
  and by little else. In the third the sentence never leaves the room. You
  have one or two of those. That is usually a fair estimate of the cost rather
  than cowardice, and the estimate is often correct, and the thing worth
  knowing is that kindness which risks nothing is manners. Manners are useful.
  They are a different instrument.

Queen of Lunaria · Queen of Pentacles   [pentacles-queen]
keywords  provision, the spare jumper, care planned ahead
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. A spare jumper comes out of your bag the minute the wind turns. You packed
  it four hours ago for someone who had not asked. A bloom above one pod. Care
  like this happens hours before the moment that makes it look obvious.
    Q. What did you pack for someone else?

 2. Six petals open above a seed pod. The provisioning happened on Tuesday. The
  gratitude happened on Saturday, if it came at all. You thought ahead about a
  body that was not yours. That is the least theoretical form of love
  available.
    Q. Whose comfort did you plan for?

 3. You carry the bag with the extra things in it. Plasters, a jumper, the
  second bottle of water. A bloom above one pod. None of it dramatic.
  Practical care is the form that gets thanked least and missed most.
    Q. What is in your bag today?

REVERSED

 1. Your jumper came out. They took it. The exchange lasted four seconds. Care
  that lands does not have to be noticed to have worked. Warm arms on a cold
  platform. That was the whole transaction.
    Q. Who took it without comment?

 2. Your bag weighs nine kilos. Most of it is for other people. Anticipating
  every wind means carrying every jumper. A person can spend a decade being
  prepared on behalf of adults who own coats. Provision has a limit and yours
  is behind you.
    Q. Who already owns a coat?

 3. The bag holds one of everything. All of it for other people. Your own hands
  are cold on the platform. Provisioning outward is a groove worn deep enough
  that reversing it feels like a stunt. There is room in the bag for a second
  jumper.
    Q. Where is your jumper?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The wind turns on the platform and a jumper comes out of a bag, and the
  timing looks like intuition. It was Tuesday. You stood in a hallway four
  hours earlier and thought about a body that was not yours, and put a spare
  in a bag for a person who had not asked and would not think to. That is the
  entire mechanism, and it is duller and better than intuition. Lunaria makes
  its pods slowly and holds seed through a whole winter. Practical care is the
  least theoretical version of the thing, and it gets thanked at a rate of
  roughly one time in six, because a need met before it arrives does not feel
  like a rescue. It feels like weather that happened to be mild. The bag is
  heavy for a reason. Most people carrying one have never mentioned it.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  The heavy bag has three states. In the first it simply works. The jumper
  comes out, it gets taken, the exchange lasts four seconds, and care that
  lands does not require acknowledgement in order to have been care. That
  version is common and almost never recorded anywhere. In the second the
  provisioning has outrun the need. Nine kilos, one of everything, carried on
  behalf of adults who own coats and can buy water, and the habit has stopped
  being generous and become a way of being necessary. That one is hard to hear
  and worth hearing. In the third the direction has never once reversed.
  Everyone on the platform is warm and the person with the bag has cold hands,
  not from martyrdom but because provisioning outward is a groove and no one
  has thought to run it the other way in a decade. There is room in your bag
  for a second jumper.

King of Hawthorn · King of Wands   [wands-king]

UPRIGHT

 1. Three seed heads on a crown. One fire lit instead of four. You declined two

  appetite.
    Q. What did you decline this week?

 2. A crown of three seed heads above one budded sprig. You skipped the meeting.
  The work happened anyway. Twenty years ago you would have gone and taken

  that long.
    Q. Which room did you not need?

 3. You have four fires you could light and the fuel for two. That used to feel

  this age is largely a list of things you stopped doing.
    Q. Where is your fuel actually going?

REVERSED

 1. You lit two and let the other two go out, on purpose. The two you lit are
  further along than four would have been. Choosing costs something real. The
  unlit ones stay interesting. They are also still unlit. That is the price of
  the aim.
    Q. Which two did you let go out?

 2. The fire went out. You called it wisdom. There is a version of this card
  where nothing gets lit. It gets called discipline. Restraint and depletion
  look identical from outside. From inside, one of them is restful.
    Q. Resting, or stalled?

 3. Four fires, all lit. You tending them in rotation. Appetite without
  subtraction is how a builder ends up maintaining instead of building. Every
  one of the four was a good idea. That is what makes this hard to see from
  the middle.
    Q. How many fires are you tending?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  A crown of three seed heads above one budded sprig, and no fourth head
  anywhere on the plate. The seasoned form of this suit is not more drive. It
  is drive that learned subtraction. Four things could be lit and there is
  fuel for two, and the younger version of the same person lit all four and
  tended them in rotation for a decade. That felt like ambition and functioned
  as maintenance. What changes with time is not appetite. It is the
  willingness to let a good idea stay unlit, which is much harder than
  declining a bad one, because a bad idea declines itself. Hawthorn does not
  flower on every branch and the branches it skips are not failures. The
  meetings you no longer attend are the visible part of this. The list of
  things you stopped doing is the actual instrument.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  There are three states here, and only one of them is aim. The first is the
  deliberate two. Two lit, two allowed to go out, and the pair you lit sits
  further along than four would have, while the unlit pair stays interesting
  for years at no cost. The second is restraint that is actually depletion.
  The fire went out on its own and the word wisdom got applied afterwards, and
  from outside the two states are the same picture, which is why no one else
  ever settles the question. Rest and stall feel different from inside. The
  third is the full four. All lit, all tended in rotation, none of them a
  mistake, and that is precisely the difficulty, because a person can spend
  fifteen years maintaining four good ideas and building none of them.
  Subtraction is the last thing this suit learns.

King of Bellflower · King of Cups   [cups-king]
keywords  composure, the count of three, feeling held
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You heard the crash and counted to three before standing. Nothing about that
  is indifference. Composure is a delay you install on purpose. It fills two
  seconds. The useful version of you is not available in them.
    Q. What happens in your three seconds?

 2. A crown of three seed heads above one hanging bell. The steady one in a
  family is not the one who feels least. Three seconds is a long time inside
  your own chest. Outside, nothing appears to happen.
    Q. Who taught you the three seconds?

 3. Three seed heads on a crown, evenly weighted. You arrived at the doorway
  calm. The room organised itself around that. Feeling was not absent from the
  count. It was held for three seconds. That is all composure has ever been.
    Q. Where do you do the holding?

REVERSED

 1. You counted to three and then went in warm. The crash turned out to be a
  bowl. Composure that is not coldness is the rarest instrument in the suit.
  You had it available on a Tuesday. It went unremarked.
    Q. What did the calm make possible?

 2. The three seconds became thirty and then became a policy. Composure kept
  past its purpose stops being composure. A crown above a bell. The steady one
  in your house has become the unreachable one. Level is not the same as
  present.
    Q. Whose calm is it serving now?

 3. You stood up on the first sound. It came out loud. The count is not always
  available. A family where it usually is can absorb a Tuesday where it was
  not. One raised voice does not undo a decade of the count.
    Q. When did the count last fail?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Something breaks in the next room and there is a gap before anyone stands.
  In the gap, a person who has done this before counts to three. The count is
  not composure. The count is the machinery composure runs on, and its purpose
  is to get past the two seconds during which the available version of you is
  loud, fast and no use to a crying child or a startled adult. What the room
  sees afterwards is a calm arrival. What actually happened was a decision
  taken in the dark, by someone who felt the same jolt as everybody else and
  declined to arrive at the same speed as it. Bellflower holds its shape in
  wind. Composure with no feeling under it reads as cold and does different
  work in a house. The three seconds are not distance. They are the opposite
  of it.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  The count goes three ways. The good one is mundane and nearly invisible.
  Three seconds, then a warm arrival, and the crash turns out to be a bowl,
  and the whole event leaves no trace except a household that trusts whoever
  stands in the doorway. Usually you. The second is the count that outgrew its
  job. Three seconds becomes thirty and then becomes a standing policy, and
  level is not the same as present, and a family can end up with a steady
  figure it can no longer reach. That state is easy to defend and hard to see,
  because it looks exactly like the virtue it started as. The third is the
  count that failed. First sound, straight up, loud, and a decade of the other
  thing is not undone by one Tuesday. Composure is a practice, and practices
  have an error rate.

keywords  stewardship, enough, doors left open
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

 1. The barn is full and the door is not locked. Both facts at once. Security
  that stays open is a decision taken after the harvest is in. It is also the
  only kind worth the name. You have enough, and your door is open.
    Q. Who else eats from your barn?

 2. A crown of three seed heads above one round pod. Full stores. Gleaners in
  the field, with permission. You gave something away this month without

  out.

 3. You have more than you need. The arithmetic of it stopped frightening you
  years ago. A crown above one pod, doors drawn open. Enough is a state, not
  an amount. Holding it loosely took more nerve than getting it did.
    Q. When did the fear go?

REVERSED

 1. Doors open, barn full. Three people took what they needed without discussing
  it. You lost nothing you can name or measure. Trust of that shape comes back
  on a schedule no one publishes. It comes back.
    Q. What came back that you never chased?

 2. Your barn is full and the door is bolted. Enough arrived and the fear
  stayed. The fear was never about the amount. A locked store is still a full
  store. It is lonelier to sit in.
    Q. What is the bolt for?

 3. The doors are open. The barn is emptier than you have admitted. Generosity
  with no floor under it is a slow transfer rather than stewardship. The
  gleaners cannot see the level. They are not being careless. Only the keeper
  reads the level.
    Q. Where is the floor?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The harvest is in the barn and the barn door is not locked. Those two facts
  sit together, and most people manage only the first. Getting enough is a
  long piece of work with a clear finish. Holding it without bolting the door
  is a separate piece of work with no finish at all, because the fear that
  arrived during the shortage does not leave when the shortage does. You
  dismiss it on purpose, repeatedly, checking the store, finding it full, and
  deciding again to leave the door as it is. Lunaria holds seed all winter and
  then drops it in the wind. Gleaners in a field with permission are not a
  charity arrangement. They are the mark of a keeper who has done the
  arithmetic and found it survivable. Enough is a state rather than a number,
  and it is held loosely or not held at all.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  A full barn goes three ways. In the first the doors stay open, three people
  take what they need without any conversation about it, the keeper loses
  nothing that can be named, and trust of that shape comes back on a schedule
  no one publishes. The second is the bolt. Enough arrived and the fear
  stayed, because it was never about the amount, and a locked store is a full
  store that is lonelier to sit in. That state is entirely defensible and it
  mostly costs the keeper. The third is the open door with no floor under it.
  Generosity beyond capacity is a slow transfer rather than stewardship, and
  the gleaners in the field cannot see the level and are not being careless in
  failing to. Only the keeper can read the level. Reading it is part of your
  job rather than a retreat from it.
