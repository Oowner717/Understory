# Gate 1 — the voice bake-off (Work Order v3 §1)

Three cards × five voices, one reading line + one question each, grouped by
card. Block F is the generic-competitor baseline: clearly labeled, never
enters the corpus, deliberately contains banned vocabulary. voice-lint scans
only drafts/*.json, so this file is exempt by construction.

Awaiting: a single letter, or a mix ("B's rhythm, C's imagery, D's endings").
Then VOICE-SPEC.md gets written with the winning samples embedded verbatim.

Note: lint word bounds (readingLine 40–55w) predate this exercise; Voice D
breaks them by design. VOICE-SPEC retunes bounds to the chosen voice.

## Card 1 · Four of Bellflower (Four of Cups)

A — Field notes
  Three cups in reach, all full, none touched. A fourth entering at the edge of the plate, easy to miss. The subject faces away. Recorded behavior: wanting, aimed past the available. You've been surveying the middle distance for a while now. The near table goes unlogged.
  Q: What within arm's reach has not been counted lately?

B — Plain and warm
  Three perfectly good cups, and you're staring past them like they owe you an apology. It happens; a full table can be the least interesting thing in the room. The fourth cup is being offered right now, by the way. From your blind side, as usual.
  Q: What's on offer this week that arrived from your blind side?

C — Literary
  Rain fills whatever is left out. Three cups stand full at your elbow, and still the eye drifts to the sky, waiting on different weather. Wanting has its own tide, and it's out just now — the shore it uncovers is long, grey, and yours to walk.
  Q: When the tide of wanting went out, what did it uncover?

D — Sparse and severe
  Three cups stand full. You look past them. A fourth is arriving; you haven't seen it. Nothing here is missing. Something here is unwanted, and it is not the cups.
  Q: What would be enough, if enough were the aim?

E — Anecdotal
  There's a plate of food going cold in front of someone scrolling for somewhere to eat. That's this card. Three cups poured, one arriving, and your attention out the window on principle. The remarkable thing isn't the boredom — it's the timing. Offers pick moments like this.
  Q: What went cold in front of you this week while you browsed?

## Card 2 · The Tower

A — Field notes
  Specimen: one tree, struck. Tallest in its stand; driest, on inspection. The bolt used a path present since planting. Limb down, trunk standing, ground line intact. Weather event: seconds. Growth of what fell: years. Your notes on the site can be brief and still true.
  Q: What did the strike find that inspection had been skipping?

B — Plain and warm
  Something big came down this week, fast, and no, it isn't secretly fine. Things you built are on the ground. But lightning only ever finds what was already tall and dry — the falling took seconds; the drying took longer, and you mostly knew.
  Q: What did you already know about the thing that fell?

C — Literary
  Lightning is the sky taking a photograph: one exposure, everything true at once. The tower shows up in it as it actually was — braced, dry, leaning into its own story. What burns afterward is the film's business. What you keep is the picture, and the cleared, ringing air.
  Q: In the flash-photograph of this collapse, what shows up plainly?

D — Sparse and severe
  It fell. It was always falling; the lightning just kept the appointment. The ground held. You're standing on it.
  Q: What is still standing, including you?

E — Anecdotal
  A neighbour once watched her shed collapse under the first serious snow and said, well, that settles the shed question. This is that card, at scale. The falling is loud and fast; the question it settles had been open, and expensive, for years. You've been paying it quarterly.
  Q: Which long-open question did this collapse just settle?

## Card 3 · Eight of Lunaria (Eight of Pentacles)

A — Field notes
  Eight pods on one stem, near-identical. Near. The third shows a cleaner seam than the first; the eighth, cleaner still. No single specimen impresses. The series does. You've repeated something enough times this month that the difference is now measurable, if unphotogenic.
  Q: Where is attempt eight cleaner than attempt one, measurably?

B — Plain and warm
  The same small thing again, slightly better — that's the whole card, and it's less boring than it sounds. Nobody claps for rep eight. But your hands have started doing a thing your head used to manage, and that handover is the actual event.
  Q: What can your hands do now that needed thinking in spring?

C — Literary
  A mint does not strike one perfect coin; it strikes thousands, and the die wears into exactness. Eight pods on the stem, each a pressing. The early ones carry burrs. The late ones carry fewer. Skill is the die, and every unglamorous strike of your week is cutting it truer.
  Q: Which repetition this week wore the die a little truer?

D — Sparse and severe
  Again. Then again. The eighth is better than the first. Not good — better. That's the mechanism. You are inside it.
  Q: Better than last time, or not?

E — Anecdotal
  Somewhere a kid is playing the same four bars for the fortieth time, and bar three finally stopped buckling. That's tonight's card. No recital, no audience — just the fortieth time being different from the thirty-ninth in a way only the player registers. You're the player in something. Bar three is worth a listen.
  Q: Which bar stopped buckling this week, and did you register it?
