UNDERSTORY — BATCH 4 REVIEW  (the junior courts, 8 cards)
============================================================================

Eight cards at full variant depth. Three reading lines and three questions
upright, three and three reversed, plus a library entry each way and alt text.
Mark each card accept / edit / recast. Edits can be written inline.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page of Hawthorn · Page of Wands   [wands-page]
keywords  curiosity, eagerness, a green shoot
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You checked out a book three subjects away from your field and cannot defend
  the choice. Good. A sprout above one sprig, and no plan anywhere on the
  plate. Curiosity that has to justify itself stops being curiosity.
    Q. What are you reading for no reason?

 2. A sprout, unfolding. Above it a budded branch. Something has your attention
  this week for no productive reason. You keep calling it a distraction. It
  has not offered a defence, because young interests do not come with one.
    Q. Who else finds it interesting?

 3. Interest arrives before its use. Always in that order. The page is the suit
  at its least strategic and most alive. Whatever you read around the edges of
  your actual work is the part you would do unpaid.
    Q. What would you do unpaid?

REVERSED

 1. You followed it and it went nowhere useful. That was the arrangement.
  Sprouts are not promises. A fortnight spent on something that led nowhere is
  not a fortnight anybody stole from you. Some following is its own return.
    Q. Where did the fortnight actually go?

 2. The book went back unread. Three renewals, good intentions, and a shelf that
  has been holding your curiosity hostage since spring. Interest keeps for a
  while and not forever. That one had a date on it.
    Q. Which one had a date on it?

 3. You have twelve interests open and none of them past the sprout. Enthusiasm
  at this stage is genuinely pleasant, and it is also where this suit can idle
  indefinitely. Budding is one thing. Growing is another.
    Q. Sprouting, or growing?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Curiosity does not arrive with a business case and it does not survive being
  asked for one. The library book three subjects sideways, the documentary
  about a trade you have no plans to practise, the fifteen minutes spent on
  the etymology of a word you did not need. All of it looks like leakage from
  a productive week, and most of it is the only part of the week that was
  actually yours. Hawthorn buds early and this is the suit's youngest form, a
  sprout above a branch, out ahead of any use. What tends to happen next is a
  small internal interrogation. The interest gets asked what it is for, it has
  no answer, and it gets filed as procrastination and dropped. There is no
  fault in a thing you cannot justify. Most of what you know well began
  exactly here, in a week somebody else would have called wasted.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three ways young interest ends and only one of them is a loss. The first is
  the honest dead end. You followed it, it went nowhere useful, and that was
  always the arrangement rather than a failure of the following. A fortnight
  spent on something with no outcome is not a fortnight anybody took from you.
  The second is expiry. Curiosity keeps for a while and not indefinitely, and
  a book renewed three times has usually already been read as much as it ever
  gets read. Some interests have a season and the season closes quietly. The
  third is the crowded shelf. Twelve things open, all of them pleasant, none
  of them past the sprout, because the sprouting is the enjoyable part and the
  growing asks for a duller kind of attention. Nobody has to choose. It is
  worth knowing which stage you like.

Page of Bellflower · Page of Cups   [cups-page]
keywords  whimsy, small messages, softness
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. A meme sent at midnight, saying the thing neither of you says. A sprout
  above one bell. Whimsy is how feeling introduces itself when the direct
  version would be too much. It counted, and you both know it.
    Q. Who did you send it to?

 2. You sent something silly to somebody this week and it was not silly. Small
  odd gestures carry more than their weight. The page is the suit's least
  dignified messenger and reliably its most effective one. Dignity was never
  the requirement.
    Q. What did the joke actually say?

 3. It looked like a joke when you sent it. It was a message. A sprout above a
  hanging bell. Something small and real got moved between two people, at an
  hour when defences are down. Neither party has to admit anything.
    Q. What small odd impulse have you dismissed?

REVERSED

 1. You sent it and they understood. No part of it had to be said plainly, which
  was the point. What needed to arrive arrived by the least direct route
  available. Whimsy delivered again. Both of you kept your cover.
    Q. What arrived without being said?

 2. The impulse got talked out of. You drafted something small and odd. Looked
  at it, decided it was too much or not enough, and put the phone down. That
  message would have landed. It kept nobody company.
    Q. What did you draft and not send?

 3. Everything you send arrives as a joke now. The sideways route stopped being
  one route and became the only one. A person who can only be sincere in a
  costume eventually cannot be sincere. That is a slow problem.
    Q. When did you last say it plainly?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  A sprout above a hanging bell, which is the youngest thing in the suit and
  the least respectable. The page of this suit does not arrive with a speech.
  It arrives as a photograph of a dog that looks like your father, sent at
  eleven at night, carrying the entire content of a conversation neither of
  you has the equipment for. Whimsy is not the opposite of depth. It is
  depth's delivery service, and it works because it gives both parties an
  exit. Nobody has to acknowledge what was actually sent. The suit's older
  cards handle feeling that has found its words, and this one handles feeling
  that has not, which is most feeling most of the time. What gets dismissed
  here is the impulse. Small, odd, undignified, and almost always the accurate
  instrument for the job. Yours are worth trusting.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three ways the indirect route goes. The first works, and works well.
  Something small and odd gets sent, it lands, both people understand it, and
  nobody has to acknowledge what was actually transmitted. That exit is a
  feature and not a cowardice. The second is the unsent draft. Written,
  examined, judged too much or not enough, and put down, and the specific loss
  is unmeasurable because a message that never went cannot report on what it
  would have done. Most people have a small archive of these. The third is the
  costume that stopped coming off. When every sincere thing has to arrive
  dressed as a joke, the joke stops being a route and becomes the only
  address, and your sincerity gets harder to perform each year it goes
  unpractised. That one responds to a plain sentence said badly rather than to
  better jokes.

Page of Gladiolus · Page of Swords   [swords-page]
keywords  watchfulness, questions, quick wit
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. A young magpie is testing every latch in your garden. It has no theory. It
  has a method, which is to try each one and watch what happens. A sprout
  above one blade, and questions with feathers.
    Q. Which latch did you try this week?

 2. Watchfulness is wit before it has had practice. You have asked a great many
  questions this week and answered almost none of them. That is the correct
  order for this stage. Latches first. Theories later.
    Q. What did you notice that nobody mentioned?

 3. You noticed something nobody else in the room noticed and said it out loud.
  A sprout above a blade leaf. That is the page's entire job description, and
  it is more useful than it feels in the moment.
    Q. Who taught you to look?

REVERSED

 1. You asked the question and it turned out to be the right one. Nobody in the
  room had checked. A young magpie found the latch open, and the whole
  afternoon changed on a question that cost you eleven words.
    Q. What did eleven words change?

 2. The questions have become the activity. You have researched, read, asked,
  and circled. Every one of those was genuinely informative. None of them was
  the thing. Noticing can be a very well-lit way of not moving.
    Q. Where has research replaced moving?

 3. You tried the latch and something broke. Curiosity at this stage has no
  sense of what is load-bearing. A fair question asked in the wrong room does
  real damage. Not malice. Inexperience, which nobody gets to skip.
    Q. Whose room was it, exactly?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  A sprout above a single blade leaf, and a magpie somewhere off the plate
  working through the latches one at a time. The page of Gladiolus is the suit
  before it has opinions. All eyes, all questions, no position to defend,
  which makes it the most useful and least respected member of the court.
  Older Gladiolus cards know things. This one finds things out, and finding
  out is a separate skill that gets lost as soon as knowing becomes available.
  What it does well is notice, which is what you did this week. The detail
  nobody mentioned, the sentence that did not fit, the latch that turns out to
  be unlocked. What it does badly is conclude, and it is not supposed to.
  Trouble only starts when the noticing becomes a substitute for acting on any
  of it, which is a later problem than this card.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three things happen to the questioner and two of them are worth the trouble.
  The first is the good version. A question gets asked that nobody in the room
  had thought to ask, it costs eleven words, and an afternoon reorganises
  itself around the answer. That is the entire value of not yet knowing
  things. The second is displacement. Reading, asking, circling and
  researching are all genuinely informative and can absorb a year, and a very
  well-lit room is still a room you have not left. The third is breakage.
  Questions have no sense of what is load-bearing, and one asked honestly in
  the wrong company at the wrong hour takes something down that was holding
  weight. That is inexperience rather than malice, nobody gets to skip it, and
  the repair afterwards is usually shorter than the dread about it.

Page of Lunaria · Page of Pentacles   [pentacles-page]
keywords  study, diligence, small savings
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. One page a day, filled by you, for eleven weeks. A sprout above one pod.
  Diligence is curiosity that shows up on schedule, and the schedule is doing
  more work than the curiosity is. Both are needed. Only one is reliable.
    Q. What have you kept going without noticing?

 2. You have kept a small thing going for longer than you have noticed. A page,
  a stretch, ten minutes, some unremarkable daily item. Nobody has praised it
  because there is nothing yet to praise. There is however a stack.
    Q. How many days is it now?

 3. A sprout above a seed pod, both drawn plainly. Like the page of this suit,
  you study. The pace is that of somebody who intends to still be here in a
  year. Slow is the strategy. Not the limitation.
    Q. Who else knows you do it?

REVERSED

 1. The streak broke on a Thursday. You started again on the Friday. That is the
  actual skill, and it is not the streak. Nobody who keeps anything for years
  has an unbroken record of it.
    Q. When did you start again?

 2. Your streak became the point. Eleven weeks of pages, and the pages have
  stopped containing anything. The requirement is now the mark on the
  calendar, not the ten minutes it was meant to protect. The ticks are
  immaculate. The pages are empty.
    Q. What is on the pages lately?

 3. You are studying instead of doing. A page a day about the thing, for eleven
  weeks, and no version of the thing itself yet. Preparation is genuinely
  pleasant and it is not the same as the work.
    Q. Studying, or doing?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Diligence has a bad name because it photographs badly. The notebook with one
  page filled every day for eleven weeks produces nothing worth showing at
  week eleven, and it is the reason somebody is competent at week two hundred.
  Lunaria takes a season and gives no weekly report, and this is the youngest
  card in the suit that already understands that. What makes the page unusual
  is that the schedule is carrying the curiosity rather than the reverse.
  Interest is unreliable and turns up when it likes. A daily slot turns up
  whether or not interest does, and the slot is what accumulates. There is no
  drama available here and none required. You, keeping a small ordinary thing
  going, unpraised because there is not yet anything to praise, quietly
  building the one asset that compounds without supervision. Nobody supervises
  it, which is exactly why it holds.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three failures of the daily habit, and the first is not a failure at all. A
  streak breaks on a Thursday and gets restarted on the Friday, and the
  restarting is the skill rather than the streak, because nobody who has kept
  anything for years has an unbroken record of it. The people who look
  consistent are the people who resumed quickly. The second is the calendar
  mark becoming the requirement. Eleven weeks of pages that no longer contain
  anything, kept because the row of ticks now has to be defended, and the ten
  minutes your habit existed to protect have quietly been evicted by the
  habit. The third is study as a substitute. A page a day about the work,
  faithfully, and no version of the work. Preparation is pleasant, visible,
  and available in unlimited quantities to anyone avoiding a first attempt.

Knight of Hawthorn · Knight of Wands   [wands-knight]
keywords  dash, appetite, boldness
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. Your reply-all went at a gallop and it covered enormous distance. It also
  took a corner badly. A chevron above one sprig. Speed is a real asset and it
  has never once improved anybody's judgement of tone.
    Q. Which line would you take back?

 2. A double chevron above a budded branch, and hoofprints going off the plate.
  You sent something fast this week, mostly correctly, and there is one line
  in it you would take back. That ratio is the knight's career.
    Q. Who else was moving at all?

 3. You moved before you were ready and it mostly worked. Nobody else in the
  thread was moving at all, which is the part the corner-taking pays for. A
  chevron above a sprig. Boldness costs something and the cost is affordable.
    Q. What did the speed buy you?

REVERSED

 1. You slowed one message down and the difference was one sentence. Same reply,
  sent an hour later, minus the line that would have needed apologising for.
  An hour is the whole technique. The message was already good. The hour only
  removed one line.
    Q. What did the extra hour remove?

 2. Your gallop went into a wall. Something got sent fast that needed to be sent
  carefully, and the recovery is taking longer than the caution would have.
  That sum is obvious afterwards and invisible before.
    Q. How long is the recovery taking?

 3. The horse has not moved. Not in a fortnight. All that momentum of yours,
  parked, because the last corner went badly and now every message gets
  drafted three times. Caution learned from one bruise overcorrects reliably.
  The thread has noticed, and it has not said so.
    Q. Parked, or resting?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  A running chevron above a budded sprig, and hoofprints leaving the frame.
  The knight of this suit is the deck's fastest thing and the least careful,
  and both halves of that are load-bearing. Distance gets covered. Corners get
  taken badly. What the card refuses to do is choose between those, because
  when you send the fast reply you are also the only person in the thread who
  sent anything, and the thread would still be open otherwise. Speed is an
  asset with a known defect, and the defect is always tone. Judgement does not
  improve with velocity, it just arrives sooner, and on a busy Thursday the
  judgement available at speed is not the best judgement in the building. The
  repair is not slowing down permanently. It is knowing which of your messages
  have corners. Most of them have none.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three states, and the middle one is where most of the learning is. The good
  one is a single hour of delay. Same message, same nerve, sent later, minus
  the one line that would have needed apologising for, and the whole technique
  fits in that hour. The second is the wall. Something went fast that needed
  to go carefully, and the recovery costs more than the caution would have,
  which is obvious afterwards and genuinely invisible beforehand, because
  velocity feels like competence right up to the corner. The third is the
  overcorrection. One bruise, and then a fortnight of a parked horse and
  messages drafted three times, and you, who used to be the only one moving,
  are now the slowest thing in the thread. Caution learned from a single event
  overcorrects reliably, and it wears off if nobody makes a policy out of it.

Knight of Bellflower · Knight of Cups   [cups-knight]
keywords  invitation, courtship, approach
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You bought the flowers and then had to carry them home on the bus. That
  second part is the card. Romance of any kind runs on nerve, and the nerve is
  required for the carrying rather than the buying.
    Q. What are you carrying home on the bus?

 2. A chevron above a hanging bell. Somebody on a bus, holding something absurd.
  You made an approach this week and it was slightly ridiculous in transit.
  Ridiculous in transit is the normal condition of any real offer.
    Q. Who did you approach?

 3. You made an offer. Now you are between the making and the answer, holding
  it, in public. The knight of this suit lives entirely in that stretch. None
  of it is dignified, and it is where everything actually happens.
    Q. What made it feel ridiculous?

REVERSED

 1. You carried it the whole way and handed it over. The answer is separate.
  Genuinely separate, because what you did was the carrying, and that part is
  complete whichever way the answer goes. The carrying was the part that took
  nerve.
    Q. What is complete, regardless of the answer?

 2. It got downgraded halfway home. Sincere when you sent it, wearing quotation
  marks when it arrived, which protects the sender and cancels the offer. The
  person receiving it can usually tell, and they almost always let it pass.
    Q. Where did the sincerity get hedged?

 3. The gesture keeps getting made. It never lands. Flowers every week, to
  nobody in particular. The making is the enjoyable part, and an actual answer
  would end the pleasant open state. You know which of yours are which.
    Q. Offering, or being seen to offer?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Every genuine approach has an undignified middle. The decision is quick and
  slightly exhilarating, the answer arrives eventually, and between them is a
  stretch of carrying the thing in public with no cover story available.
  Flowers on a bus. A message sent and visibly delivered. An invitation
  extended and not yet answered. The knight of Bellflower lives in exactly
  that stretch, and the nerve this card measures is not the nerve required to
  offer, which is brief. It is the nerve required to keep holding the offer
  while it is conspicuous and unanswered. Most people can decide. Fewer can
  carry. That is why so many approaches get withdrawn in the middle, converted
  retroactively into jokes, or hedged into ambiguity that protects the offerer
  and removes the offer. The bus ride is short. It only feels long because
  everyone can see you.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three ways the carrying goes. In the first it simply completes. The thing
  gets carried the whole way and handed over, and the answer becomes a
  separate matter, genuinely separate, because what you did was the carrying,
  and that is finished whichever way the answer falls. That distinction is
  worth more than it sounds. The second is the retroactive joke. An offer
  leaves sincere and arrives in quotation marks, which protects the sender
  exactly as much as it cancels the offer, and the person on the other end can
  nearly always tell and nearly always lets it pass. The third is the gesture
  that never lands on purpose. Offers made repeatedly, warmly, to nobody in
  particular, because the offering is the enjoyable part and a real answer
  would close a pleasant open state. All three are ordinary. Only one of them
  is an approach.

Knight of Gladiolus · Knight of Swords   [swords-knight]
keywords  haste, argument, the charge
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. The squall crossed the valley in ten minutes. Loud, fast, gone. The fence is
  down. Some arguments work like weather, and afterwards the sensible question
  is about the fence rather than the clouds. You were somewhere in that ten
  minutes.
    Q. What is down that was standing on Monday?

 2. You were right. You were also the weather. A chevron above one blade,
  moving. Speed of thought is not depth of it. A case made at full volume gets
  remembered for the volume, long after the case is granted.
    Q. Who was in the way?

 3. Something got said fast. It landed hard. You charged, which is what the
  knight of this suit does, which is useful when the thing genuinely needed
  charging at. Which of those this was shows up in the fence line rather than
  in the argument.
    Q. Right, or just loud?

REVERSED

 1. Your squall passed. The fence got mended the same week. Fast damage repaired
  fast leaves almost no trace, and the speed that caused it is the same speed
  that fixed it. That symmetry is the saving grace.
    Q. What got mended this week?

 2. Every conversation of yours has become weather. The charge that worked once
  is now the only gear. A person who arrives at speed in every room stops
  being listened to and starts being waited out. Waiting out is cheaper than
  arguing.
    Q. Who waits you out now?

 3. The charge never left the gate. You have the case, you have the nerve, and
  it has been Thursday for three weeks. Held charges sour, and they come out
  later at the wrong volume about the wrong thing.
    Q. What has been Thursday for weeks?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  A chevron above a single blade, both angled as though already in motion. The
  knight of Gladiolus is the suit at speed, which for a suit made of edges
  means arguments arriving before anyone has agreed to have one. The charge is
  often correct. That is the part people miss when they dismiss this card,
  because someone has to say the unwelcome accurate thing, and this week it
  was you and the polite room never produces it. What the charge costs is
  separate from whether it was right. Volume gets remembered longer than
  content, and a case won at speed gets granted and resented in the same
  afternoon. Weather is the honest comparison. The squall does real work,
  clears real air, and takes down a fence that was holding something, and the
  fence is the part worth counting afterwards. Fences are cheaper to count
  than to argue about.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three weathers. The first is the honest squall, arriving fast, doing damage
  and work in the same ten minutes, and mended the same week, because the
  speed that took the fence down is the identical speed that puts it back.
  Almost no trace remains, and the whole event reads afterwards as a bad
  afternoon rather than a change in the climate. The second is the single
  gear. A charge worked once, decisively, and became the only available
  approach, and a person who arrives at volume in every room, which may be you
  lately, stops being answered and starts being waited out, which is cheaper
  for everyone else and invisible to them. The third is the charge held at the
  gate. Case ready, nerve ready, three weeks of Thursdays, and held charges
  sour in storage. They come out eventually at the wrong volume, about the
  wrong thing, at somebody who was not the target.

Knight of Lunaria · Knight of Pentacles   [pentacles-knight]
keywords  routine, thoroughness, the long row
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You hoed the whole row, including the middle, where nobody was watching and
  nothing was interesting. A chevron above one pod. Thoroughness is
  unglamorous until it turns out to be the entire difference. Nobody is
  standing at the middle of a row.
    Q. Which middle are you in?

 2. The middle of the row is where most rows get abandoned. Not the start, which
  is exciting, and not the end, which is visible. The middle, which is
  neither. You have been in a middle lately and you are still hoeing.
    Q. What did you finish that nobody saw?

 3. A chevron above a seed pod. Both plain, neither hurried. Like the knight of
  this suit, you are the slowest thing in the court and the one that finishes.
  Reliability looks unremarkable from outside. From inside it looks like a
  finished row.
    Q. Who relies on you for this?

REVERSED

 1. You finished the row. Nobody mentioned it. That is standard, and not a
  slight. Finished rows do not announce themselves, and the person who notices
  them is usually the one who hoed. You noticed. That is the usual
  arrangement.
    Q. What did you finish unremarked?

 2. The row stopped two thirds along. Not from laziness, from the middle, which
  is where rows stop. You could pick it up where it stopped. That point is
  closer to the end than the abandoning made it feel.
    Q. Where did the row stop?

 3. You are hoeing a row nobody needs finished. Thoroughness with no judgement
  attached hoes anything, including a field planted by mistake, and does it
  beautifully, all season. Diligence has no opinion about direction. Nobody
  checks the direction of a good worker.
    Q. Which row does not need finishing?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The middle of the row is where things get abandoned. Beginnings recruit
  attention and endings are visible from a distance, and between them is a
  stretch that is neither, in which the work is identical and the interest is
  gone. Getting through that stretch is the entire competence this card
  describes. Lunaria's knight is the slowest figure in the deck's court and
  the one that arrives, and those two facts are the same fact. What makes the
  middle hard is not difficulty. It is the absence of feedback in either
  direction, no novelty and no finish line, just the same motion in a part of
  the field nobody visits. Whoever depends on you probably depends on this
  rather than on anything you would list. Yours is invisible until the one
  occasion it is the whole difference. That occasion arrives without notice.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three ways thoroughness plays out. The first is the unremarked finish. A row
  gets completed, nobody mentions it, and that is the standard result rather
  than a slight, because finished rows are silent by nature and the only
  reliable witness is the person who hoed. The second is the two-thirds stop,
  which happens in the middle and not from laziness, and it leaves the work
  closer to done than the abandoning made it feel. Picking up where it stopped
  is generally a smaller job than starting anything. The third is the harder
  one. Thoroughness carries no opinion about direction, so it hoes whatever
  row it is pointed at, including a field planted by mistake, and it does that
  beautifully for an entire season. Diligence is a wonderful engine and a poor
  navigator, and it very rarely asks you whether the row was the right one.
