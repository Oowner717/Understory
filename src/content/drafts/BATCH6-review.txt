Death   [M13]   [HEAVY]
keywords  an ending done, the empty bed, the ground stays
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You pulled the dead stems out. The bed is empty now. That emptiness is the
  work, finished. Something in your life ended this season and you were the
  one who cleared it. An empty bed in November is not a failed garden.
    Q. What did you clear this season?

 2. Something ended and you are standing in the space where it was. That space
  is wider than it looks in November. A bare teasel head. Seed gone, structure
  still standing. You are allowed to call it over on your own schedule.
    Q. Where are you standing now?

 3. Autumn does this to every bed in the country. Inside the same fortnight.
  Yours is not being singled out. The stems come out, the ground stays. The
  ground is the part that was always doing the work. You are still here.
    Q. What is still in the ground?

REVERSED

 1. You cleared it. You were sad about it. Both happened in the same week. That
  combination is the ordinary shape of an ending done properly. No
  contradiction there. The bed is empty and you are allowed to mind.
    Q. What are you allowed to mind?

 2. The stems are still standing in December and you know they are dead. Leaving
  them there is not denial. It is a way of not doing it yet. That is a
  different thing, and it costs something. The bed can wait.
    Q. When does the bed get cleared?

 3. You pulled everything, including two that were alive. Clearing at speed does
  not stop to check. That is what haste in autumn costs. It is a small loss
  inside a correct decision. The bed was still right.
    Q. What went out with it?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The dead stems come out in October and what is left is bare soil, and every
  year a gardener looks at that bed and reads it as failure. It is the
  opposite. The emptiness is the work, completed, on schedule, by a person who
  did the pulling. Something of yours ended this autumn. You may have ended it
  yourself, or watched it end, or spent three months knowing it was over
  before anything moved, and all three of those are endings and none of them
  is a mistake. What stays is the ground. Beds do not lose their soil when
  they lose their planting, and the part that was doing the work all along is
  still exactly where it was. This card names the difficulty and does not
  soften it. It also does not pretend the ground went anywhere. You are
  standing on it.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two things happen to an autumn clearing and neither is a moral failing.
  Either it does not happen, or it happens too fast. Stems left standing into
  December are not denial. They are a method of postponement, which takes its
  own kind of effort, and the bed waits without penalty because beds do.
  Clearing at speed is the other one, and it pulls two live plants along with
  the twelve dead ones, because haste does not stop to check. Both of those
  sit inside a decision that was still correct. What the reversal is not is a
  verdict on your timing. Autumn reaches every bed in the country within a
  fortnight of every other and consults none of them, and the ground under
  yours held through all of it. You are allowed to have minded, and for longer
  than seemed reasonable.

ALT TEXT

  A bare teasel head drawn in line, centered on the plate above the numeral.

The Devil   [M15]   [HEAVY]
keywords  a hundred harmless evenings, the tangle, privacy
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You opened it again without deciding to. That is the whole mechanism and it
  is not a character flaw. A bramble knot, grown from a hundred separate
  evenings, none of which was the problem. The knot is still a knot.
    Q. What opens without you deciding?

 2. A bramble knot, drawn in line. No beginning visible anywhere in it. None of
  this was chosen once. It was chosen on a hundred evenings, each of them
  harmless. The compound is what you are looking at. Brambles are not
  villains. They are very good at this.
    Q. Which evening would you have skipped?

 3. You know exactly which app it is and how long the average evening runs.
  Naming it is not the same as being ashamed of it. A knot has ends. Finding
  one takes longer than pulling it. Pulling is the part most people start
  with.
    Q. Where does the knot start?

REVERSED

 1. You put it in a drawer for one evening. The evening was long and fine. One
  evening is not a cure and was never offered as one. It was a demonstration.
  The knot loosened by exactly the amount you loosened it.
    Q. What did the long evening feel like?

 2. The knot is tighter than it was in spring. You stopped counting the
  evenings. Growth like this is silent and does not announce a threshold. A
  bramble does not decide to take a garden. It takes one by not being cut.
    Q. When did you stop counting?

 3. You call this a weakness. It has been getting stronger on that diet. Shame
  is not a cutting tool. It keeps the evenings private. Privacy is the one
  condition brambles actually need. A number said out loud to another person
  is no longer private.
    Q. Who knows the number?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  A bramble knot is not built on purpose. It arrives by accretion, from a
  hundred separate evenings, and not one of those evenings was the problem,
  which is exactly why the thing is hard to see and harder to date. You know
  which app it is. You could probably estimate the average evening to within
  ten minutes, and the estimate would be low, and none of that is a character
  defect. It is a compound of small permissions. Brambles are extremely good
  at this and hold no opinion about you at all. The useful fact about a knot
  is that it has ends. Locating an end takes longer than hauling on it, and
  pulling is where most attempts begin, which is why most attempts tighten it.
  This card does not tell you to cut anything. It says the knot grew the
  ordinary way, and that its main requirement is privacy.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two states, and one of them is only visible from a distance. In the first,
  you put the thing in a drawer for a single evening and the evening turns out
  to be long and fine, and a single evening cures nothing and never claimed
  to. It is a demonstration that the loosening is available. In the second,
  the tangle has thickened since spring and the evenings stopped being counted
  some months ago, because growth of this kind is silent and never announces a
  threshold. What makes the second state persist is not weakness. It is
  privacy. Shame keeps the number unsaid, and unsaid is the one condition
  brambles require, so the shame and the bramble end up on the same side. A
  figure spoken to one other person has stopped being private. That is all
  this entry claims. It is also the only part of it that costs you anything.

ALT TEXT

  A tangled bramble knot drawn in line, centered on the plate above the
  numeral.

The Moon   [M18]   [HEAVY]
keywords  the smaller room, same facts, the hour
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. At two in the morning it is enormous. At nine it is a phone call and nothing
  more. Same facts, both times. The difference is not information. Knowing
  that at nine does not help you at two.
    Q. What is it at nine?

 2. A moon on the plate and a moth in front of it. You were awake at two. The
  shape of the thing was wrong. Not exaggerated. A different animal, using the
  same facts. It had your full attention because the rest of the world was
  shut.
    Q. Which animal was it at two?

 3. You lost some sleep to something that is a manageable size in daylight. That
  is not a failure of perspective. Night has fewer objects in it. The one you
  have gets bigger. The moth is not wrong about the moon being the brightest
  thing.
    Q. Where did the size come from?

REVERSED

 1. You waited until nine and made the call and it took four minutes. The two in
  the morning version does not have to be argued with. It has to be outlasted.
  You outlasted it by sleeping badly and then getting up.
    Q. How long did it actually take?

 2. It is three in the morning. Your problem has grown a face. Fear at this hour
  is not information about the problem. It is information about the hour. The
  face belongs to the hour. It was not there at four in the afternoon.
    Q. What did it look like at four?

 3. You have started making decisions at two in the morning. That is the hazard.
  The two in the morning version is confident. It is working from a smaller
  room. Decisions taken there get taken again at nine.
    Q. What got decided at two?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The same problem has a two in the morning version and a nine in the morning
  version, and the facts are identical in both. Only the size changes, and it
  changes enormously. None of that is a lapse of perspective, and a calmer
  person would not have dodged it either. Night has fewer objects in it than
  day, so whatever is in the room takes up all of it, and a moth in the dark
  is not wrong in judging the moon the brightest object available. What the
  card names is the gap, and it declines to tell you the daylight version is
  the true one. Both readings use real facts. The night version is simply
  working in a smaller room, and with more confidence than the room supports.
  The one genuine hazard here is deciding at that hour, since a decision taken
  at two gets taken again at nine.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two ways this goes and neither of them is a lapse. In the first, you lose
  the hours and then make the call at nine and it takes four minutes, and the
  night version was never something to argue with. It was something to
  outlast, and a bad night followed by getting up is how outlasting works. In
  the second, the hour is three and the thing has grown a face, and the fear
  at that hour is information about the hour rather than about the thing. The
  face belongs to the clock. At four in the afternoon it had no face at all,
  and arriving in the dark does not make it a truer picture. What the reversal
  does not say is that the daylight reading is correct and the night one is
  hysteria. It says a smaller room makes a bigger object. Nothing about the
  object changed.

ALT TEXT

  A full moon in line, with a single moth in front of it, above the numeral.

The Tower   [M16]   [HEAVY]
keywords  fast at the end, the clearing, the ground held
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT   (Gate 1 specimen text, frozen)

 1. You built it, you maintained it, and this week you watched it come down. All
  three of those are true at once, and none of them cancels the others. What
  you knew and couldn't say is now visible from the road. That part, at least,
  is over.
    Q. What can you admit now?

 2. Something you built came down this week. It went fast at the end, after
  being slow for a long time, the way these things go. You're allowed to just
  stand here for a minute. The card stands with you. The ground under both of
  you held.
    Q. What is still standing?

 3. Trees fall in an afternoon after growing for forty years. The afternoon gets
  all the attention. The drying takes seasons and no one photographs that
  part. Only the clearing is visible from where you stand today, and it is
  honestly wide.
    Q. Where does the light reach now?

REVERSED

 1. The tree came down in April and you have been clearing since. The falling
  was the fast part. What takes the years is the drying and the hauling. Also
  the not thinking about it for whole weeks at a time. That is also progress.
    Q. What have you cleared already?

 2. The strike is old news. The clearing is done. The sky over that spot is now
  just the sky. Nothing about this is a return to before. You are standing in
  a different field. Level, and with more light in it than the old one had.
    Q. What grows there now?

 3. You are rebuilding on the same footprint and using the same measurements.
  That is allowed. It is worth knowing you are doing it. Some of the old plan
  was good. Some of it is why the tree was standing where the lightning went.
    Q. Which measurements are you reusing?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Lightning takes a fraction of a second and the tree is on the ground by the
  end of the afternoon, and that afternoon gets all the attention, including
  yours. The forty years of growing get none. Neither does the clearing, which
  runs for seasons, in loads, with long gaps where nothing gets touched at
  all. Something you built came down. You built it, you maintained it, and you
  watched it fall, and all three of those are true at the same time without
  cancelling each other. That is the part that is hardest to hold. What you
  knew and could not say is visible from the road now. That much is over. The
  ground held through it, which is a plain fact about the ground rather than a
  consolation, and it happens to be the most useful fact available. Only the
  clearing is visible from where you stand today. It is honestly wide.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  The falling is the short part. It photographs well and it is over in an
  afternoon, and everything afterwards is drying and hauling and long
  stretches of not thinking about it, which goes unrecorded as progress and
  which is exactly what progress looks like here. At some distance the strike
  becomes old news, the clearing finishes, and the sky above that gap is only
  sky, and the field that remains is not the old field restored. It is a
  different field, level, with more light in it. The one thing worth naming is
  the rebuild on the same footprint. Reusing your old measurements is
  permitted and common, and some of that old plan was good, and some of it is
  the reason the tree stood exactly where the lightning went. Both parts of
  that are true. Knowing which is which is a job for later, and later is
  allowed to be much later.

  A lightning-struck tree drawn in line, trunk split, centered above the plate
  numeral.

The Hanged Man   [M12]
keywords  the handed hour, suspension, invisible work
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. Your train is ninety minutes late and there is nothing to do about it. You
  did not choose this hour and would never have scheduled it. A chrysalis on
  the plate, hanging, doing invisible work. The hour counts anyway.
    Q. What did the delay hand you?

 2. A chrysalis, suspended and sealed. It is doing something you cannot watch.
  Waiting looks identical to nothing from outside. Your week put you somewhere
  you did not pick. Time you did not budget. You have already thought
  something you would not have thought.
    Q. What surfaced in the extra hour?

 3. Chosen stillness is a practice. Imposed stillness is a platform at Crewe
  with a coffee going cold. The second one produces more, more often. That is
  annoying and well documented. You would not have given yourself that hour.
    Q. When did you last stop on purpose?

REVERSED

 1. The delay handed you an hour and you used it. Ninety minutes. Using it
  looked like sitting still and staring at a departure board. That is what use
  looks like here. Nothing got produced and something got noticed.
    Q. What did you notice at the station?

 2. You filled the ninety minutes with your phone and arrived with the same head
  you left with. That is the ordinary outcome and it is not a moral event. The
  hour was still handed to you. It went back unopened.
    Q. Where did the ninety minutes go?

 3. The stillness has stopped being imposed and become the arrangement. Hanging
  is a stage. Not an address. A chrysalis that never opens is not resting. A
  pause you were handed is not a pause you moved into. The difference is real.
    Q. Handed to you, or moved into?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The train is ninety minutes late, the platform is cold, and there is nothing
  available to do about any of it. That is the situation this card describes,
  and the useful thing about it is that you would never have scheduled it. An
  hour given to yourself on purpose is a practice, and practices are good, and
  they are also rare and easily postponed. An hour taken from you by a
  signalling fault arrives whether or not you had planned on it. What tends to
  happen in that hour is small and hard to report. A thought surfaces that had
  been queued behind the ordinary noise, and it is usually about something you
  already knew. A chrysalis does its work sealed and unwatched and looks from
  outside like a thing hanging on a twig. You cannot photograph the middle of
  this. You can notice, afterwards, that it happened.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two ways the handed hour goes, and one edge case worth naming. It gets used,
  which looks like nothing, since using it means sitting on a bench watching a
  departure board while a thought surfaces that had been queued behind the
  noise. Or it goes back unopened, filled with a phone, and you arrive with
  the head you left with, and that is the ordinary outcome rather than a moral
  event. Neither of those is a failure and both happen to everybody. The edge
  case is when the pause stops being imposed. Hanging is a stage rather than
  an address, and a chrysalis that does not open is not resting, and the
  difference between a pause you were handed and one you have moved into is
  real and only visible from inside. That distinction is the whole of the
  reversal. Everything else here is just a late train.

ALT TEXT

  A chrysalis hanging from a twig, drawn in line, centered above the plate
  numeral.

Judgement   [M20]
keywords  the old handwriting, scale, not a scorecard
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You read an old entry and recognised the handwriting and not the worry. Same
  hand. Different animal on the page. Whatever that was about, it resolved
  without you noticing the moment it stopped mattering. Most of them do.
    Q. Which worry no longer reaches you?

 2. A cicada shell, split down the back, the animal gone. You have outgrown a
  worry this year and did not attend the moment it happened. There was no
  announcement. The evidence is that you cannot get back into the old one.
    Q. What can you no longer feel?

 3. Old journals are the only honest record of what you used to be afraid of.
  Memory edits. Paper does not. Reading your own handwriting about a worry
  that has since dissolved is the closest available look at your own scale.
    Q. Where were you a year ago?

REVERSED

 1. You read the entry and the worry had gone and you felt almost nothing about
  it. That flatness is the accurate response. Nothing to mark. A resolved
  thing does not owe you a feeling at the moment you notice it resolved.
    Q. What resolved without a ceremony?

 2. You read the entry. The worry is still current, word for word, two years on.
  That is worth knowing plainly. Some shells do not split on schedule. A worry
  that has held the same size for two years is information. Not a verdict.
    Q. Which one is still current?

 3. You reread the old entries in order to grade yourself. They are not a
  scorecard. The person who wrote them was working with what they had.
  Judgement in this card is a reading and not a sentence.
    Q. Who were you grading?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  An old journal entry is the only unedited account of your former fears,
  because memory edits continuously and paper does not. The common experience
  of rereading one is a mild vertigo. The handwriting is yours and
  unmistakable, and the worry on the page belongs to a person you can no
  longer fully reach, and there was no moment at which it stopped mattering,
  or at least no moment you attended. A cicada shell splits down the back and
  the animal is elsewhere, and nothing about the shell records the hour it
  happened. What this card offers is scale, and scale is the least dramatic
  and most useful thing on the list. It is not a verdict on the earlier
  version of you, who had less to work with than you do. It is a measurement,
  taken twice, on a person who was moving the whole time.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two readings and a misuse. In the first, the worry has gone and you feel
  almost nothing about it, and the flatness is correct rather than cold,
  because a resolved thing owes you no feeling on the day you notice it has
  gone. Nothing needs marking. In the second, the entry reads as current two
  years on, down to the wording, and that is worth stating plainly rather than
  gently. Not every shell splits on schedule, and a worry that has not shrunk
  in two years is information rather than a verdict on you. The misuse is
  rereading in order to grade. Old entries are not a scorecard, and the person
  who filled them was working with the information available at the time,
  which was less than you have now. Judgement here is a reading taken twice on
  a person who kept moving between the two. It was never a sentence.

ALT TEXT

  An empty cicada shell split down the back, drawn in line above the plate
  numeral.

Strength   [M08]
keywords  the floor at the edge of the bed, gentleness as technique, the hour it takes
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You are on the floor at the edge of the bed. The cat has not come out. Force
  has never once worked here. Not once, in the entire history of frightened
  animals. Strength here is the patience to stay on the floor.
    Q. Who are you waiting for?

 2. A small perched bird, no cage anywhere near it. Whatever you were gentle
  with this week did not respond to being pushed. You knew that before you
  tried. Gentleness is the technique and not the mood.
    Q. What did you not push?

 3. You held your temper with something that deserved it. That is the whole
  card. It is more expensive than it sounds. Force is faster and it does not
  work on frightened things. Staying on the floor is what took the strength.
    Q. Where did you hold it?

REVERSED

 1. The cat came out on its own at eleven, when you had stopped looking at it.
  Nothing you did in the last hour caused that. Everything you did in the
  first hour made it possible. Both are true.
    Q. When did it come out?

 2. You reached under the bed and got scratched. Force applied to a frightened
  animal produces exactly one result. It is on your hand. Not cruelty.
  Impatience, which is far more common and leaves the same mark.
    Q. What were you out of?

 3. Six weeks on this floor. The cat is comfortable under there now. Patience
  with no limit stops being patience. Waiting can become the thing you do
  instead of deciding. Gentleness has a version that never asks for anything.
    Q. How long have you been waiting?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  A person is flat on the carpet beside a bed, talking quietly to an animal
  that has stayed under it. That is the picture, and everything this card has
  to say is inside it. Force is faster, and force has never once worked on a
  frightened creature, and the person on the floor knows this from experience
  rather than from principle. What it costs is the hour. You spent one like
  that recently. Gentleness of this kind is not a temperament and not a mood,
  and it is not niceness. It is a technique with a known duration, applied by
  someone who would very much prefer to be doing something else. The cat comes
  out eventually, on its own schedule, usually once the room has stopped
  watching, and the last quiet hour gets no credit for that while being the
  reason it happened. Strength is the wrong word for it and the only one
  available.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two failures and one success, and the success is the least visible of the
  three. The success is the animal coming out at eleven, by itself, once the
  watching stopped, with nothing in the final hour causing it and everything
  in your first hour making it possible. Both of those are true and only one
  of them feels like a result. The first failure is the hand under the bed.
  Force applied to a frightened creature produces a single outcome and it is a
  scratch, and the cause is almost never cruelty. It is haste, which is
  commoner by far and marks a hand identically. The second failure is the
  floor becoming a residence. Endless patience is no longer patience, waiting
  stands in for deciding very comfortably, and there is a form of gentleness
  that never asks anybody for anything and calls that a virtue.

ALT TEXT

  A small bird perched on a bare twig, drawn in line above the plate numeral.

Justice   [M11]
keywords  both columns, the same ink, keeping the total
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. One column says what it cost. The other says what it paid. Same ink, same
  hand, same page. You read only one of them this week. A balance scale on the
  plate, level, holding both columns at once.
    Q. Which column did you skip?

 2. A balance scale, level, both pans loaded. Fairness is not a feeling. It is
  not a mood you arrive at. It is two columns written in the same ink by the
  same person. You did the honest version of this recently, on something
  specific.
    Q. What did it actually pay?

 3. You gave something a fair reading this week. Including the unflattering
  parts. That is rarer than being right. A scale reads level only when both
  pans are loaded honestly. The second pan is the one that gets left light.
    Q. Whose account are you keeping?

REVERSED

 1. You added up both columns and the answer was not the one you wanted. Then
  you kept it. Holding an honest total that goes against you is the entire
  content of this card. It takes about a day to stop stinging.
    Q. What total did you keep?

 2. One column is in ink and the other is in pencil. That is the ordinary way
  this gets done. It is not lying. The costs are precise and the payments
  approximate, or the reverse. Either way your scale reads level on a page
  that is not.
    Q. Which column is in pencil?

 3. Every account gets settled in your head at eleven at night. Fairness turned
  inward without limit is not justice. It is a tribunal. This scale weighs one
  decision at a time. It does not weigh a whole person.
    Q. When does the weighing stop?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Two columns, one page, the same ink in both. What it cost on the left and
  what it paid on the right, written by the same hand, at the same sitting,
  with the same care. That is the whole instrument, and almost everyone runs
  it half-loaded, because one column is easy to fill and the other is the one
  that makes the total inconvenient. Which column gets shortchanged varies by
  person and stays consistent within a person. A balance scale reads level for
  a reason, and the reason is arithmetic rather than virtue. Fairness in this
  card has nothing to do with feeling fair, and feeling fair is a poor
  indicator, since a page with one pan light also comes to a total and the
  total looks just as tidy. What is asked here is narrow. One decision, both
  columns, same ink, and then keeping the answer even when it goes against
  you.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  The honest version and the two dishonest ones, and the dishonest pair are
  not lies. In the honest version both columns get added, the answer comes out
  against you, and you keep it anyway, which stings for roughly a day and then
  stops. That is the entire achievement. In the first failure one side is
  inked and the other pencilled. Costs precise and payments approximate, or
  the reverse, and the pans still balance above a page that does not, and
  nobody involved has said anything untrue. The second failure is scale creep.
  Every account settled nightly, in the head, at eleven, with fairness turned
  all the way inward and no stopping condition, which is a tribunal rather
  than a justice. The instrument takes one decision at a time. Handing it a
  whole person is a category error, and it produces a number every time.

ALT TEXT

  A two-pan balance scale hanging level, drawn in line above the plate
  numeral.

Temperance   [M14]
keywords  a quarter teaspoon, increments, no story
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. Your eleventh version of the soup differs from the tenth by a quarter
  teaspoon. That is the whole method. It is extremely boring. Two vessels,
  joined by a stream. Nothing here arrives by a leap.
    Q. What did you adjust by a quarter?

 2. Two vessels joined by a moving stream, neither of them full. You changed one
  small thing this week. It was the right size of change. Small adjustments
  are unimpressive. They are also the only ones that hold. The good version is
  boringly near the last one.
    Q. Which small thing did you change?

 3. You resisted the urge to fix it all at once. That urge is strong. It
  produces version one again, from scratch, with new mistakes. A quarter
  teaspoon is not a compromise. It is the resolution the problem actually has.
    Q. Where were you tempted to start over?

REVERSED

 1. Version eleven works and it tastes almost exactly like version ten. You
  cannot point to the change. The change is still why it works. Increments
  make a poor story and a reliable soup. The recipe never gets asked for.
    Q. What tastes right now?

 2. You threw the whole pot out and started again from nothing. That happens. It
  is usually about frustration rather than about the recipe. Version one has a
  fresh set of mistakes in it. Some of them are the ones you already fixed.
    Q. What did you already fix once?

 3. You have adjusted eleven times now. The dish is not the problem. Increments
  are a way of moving. They are also a way of not moving. Eleven versions of
  nearly the same thing is a decision avoided. Carefully, and in a kitchen.
    Q. Adjusting, or avoiding?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The version that works differs from the previous version by a quarter
  teaspoon, and that is the least satisfying sentence in this deck. It is also
  how almost everything gets good. Two vessels with a stream between them,
  neither one full, the level moving slowly between them. What makes this hard
  is not the technique, which a child can follow. It is that increments
  produce no story. There is nothing to tell at a dinner table afterwards, no
  moment where the thing turned, and no version you can point to as the
  breakthrough, because there was not one. The competing urge is to throw the
  pot out and rebuild from scratch, which feels decisive and delivers version

  thing and tasting it before changing another.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two ways the method fails, and one of them looks like decisiveness. The
  clean case first. Version eleven works, tastes very nearly like the tenth,
  and you cannot trace it to any single change, and the untraceable change is
  precisely why it works, which is why nobody asks how it was done. The first
  failure is the fresh start. The whole pot goes out, the rebuild feels like
  action, and version one arrives with a new set of mistakes, some of them
  already solved two weeks ago, and the trigger is nearly always frustration
  rather than the dish. The second failure is subtler and more comfortable.
  Eleven quarter-teaspoon adjustments to a dish that was never the problem,
  because incrementing is a way of moving and also an excellent way of not
  moving, and both look identical from the stove. The taste is the only test
  that settles it.
