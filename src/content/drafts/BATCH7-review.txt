The Fool   [M00]
keywords  the shorter list, the door, lightness as a decision
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. Three things came out of your bag and stayed out. That is the decision, made
  at the door, in about four seconds. A feather on the plate, drawn once.
  Lightness is not innocence. It is a shorter list.
    Q. What did you leave out?

 2. A feather, one line, no weight to it at all. You left something behind on
  purpose this week. The relief was immediate and slightly suspicious.
  Beginnings are subtraction. Most people expect the opposite and pack
  accordingly.
    Q. Where did the relief come from?

 3. You are standing at the door with a lighter bag than you planned. The taxi
  does not check. The zip closed on the first try. That is the only test this
  card runs. A feather, once, in ink.
    Q. Which three would you leave out?

REVERSED

 1. You went with the short list and missed nothing. Not one of the things you
  left behind was needed. That is the ordinary result and it stays surprising.
  The bag was right. You were carrying an old estimate.
    Q. What did you not need?

 2. The bag is packed for four scenarios and the trip has one destination. You
  have repacked it twice. Preparation past a point is another word for
  reluctance. Every item has a reason. The reasons are not the problem.
    Q. How many scenarios are in there?

 3. You left too much behind and stood on a platform without a coat. Lightness
  has a floor and you found it by going through it. One cold morning. The list
  is a draft and not a principle.
    Q. Whose list was that?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Packing is subtraction, and the decision happens at the door, in the four
  seconds when three things come out of the bag and stay out. That is the
  whole of it. Beginnings get imagined as accumulation, as gathering what you
  need, and in practice the moment anything actually starts is the moment a
  list gets shorter. A feather is one line on a plate with no weight in it,
  which is a hard thing to draw and an easy thing to carry. What tends to
  follow the subtraction is a relief so immediate that it feels like a
  mistake, and then a slow week of discovering that not one of the removed
  items was wanted. Nothing at the door is being inspected. The zip closing
  first time is the only test available, and it is a better test than the list
  was.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Two ways the short list goes wrong, and one way it goes right. Right is
  unremarkable. You travel light, need none of what you left, and register
  mild surprise at an outcome that repeats every time, because the estimate in
  your head was built for a version of you from some years ago. The first
  failure is the bag packed for four scenarios when the trip has one
  destination. Every item in it can be justified, individually, and that is
  precisely the trouble, since preparation past a certain point is a different
  word for reluctance. The second failure is the platform in February with no
  coat. There is a floor under all this, and it gets located by dropping
  through it, which costs one cold morning and settles the question for years.
  The list is a draft. Treating it as a principle is how both failures start.

The Magician   [M01]
keywords  the arranged bench, readiness, starting is not a tool
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. Every tool you need is on the bench and has been for three weeks. Nothing is
  missing. A flask on the plate, clean, empty, ready. The missing tool is
  starting. It is not on the bench because it is not a tool.
    Q. What is already on your bench?

 2. A flask, drawn plainly, with a measure line and nothing in it. You have the
  materials, the skill and the afternoon. Readiness is not a stage. It is a
  place people stop. Comfortably, and with everything arranged.
    Q. Where did you stop?

 3. You arranged it all again on Sunday. Arranging is real work. It is also the
  most convincing way to not begin. The bench has been ready longer than the
  project has existed. One pour would settle it.
    Q. When was the bench last used?

REVERSED

 1. You started it on Tuesday, badly, and the bench got messy inside ten
  minutes. That mess is the difference between this card upright and reversed.
  Nothing was ready. Everything was available. That is a lower bar and the
  only real one.
    Q. What did you start badly?

 2. Your bench is arranged and photographed and the project is three months old.
  Preparation has become the project. That happens to most people and gets
  called being organised. A flask stays clean by not being used.
    Q. Which one is three months old?

 3. You bought a fourth tool this month for a job that needed none of the first
  three. Acquisition feels adjacent to progress and reliably is not. The bench
  is fuller. The afternoon is the same length.
    Q. What did the fourth tool add?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  Everything required is already laid out on the bench, and was laid out a
  fortnight before that. That is the situation, and the useful part of it is
  that nothing is missing, which removes the most popular excuse in one
  stroke. The tool that is absent is starting, and starting cannot be laid out
  with the others. A flask on a plate is clean and empty and reads as ready,
  and a flask stays clean precisely by never being used. What makes this hard
  is that arranging is genuine work. It takes skill, it produces a visible
  result, it is pleasant, and it can be repeated indefinitely on Sunday
  afternoons without the project advancing by a single hour. Readiness is not
  a stage of anything. It is somewhere people stop, and the stopping is
  comfortable, and your bench looks like evidence of the opposite.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  One good outcome and two comfortable ones. The good one looks worse than the
  alternatives. It is a Tuesday, the thing gets going badly, and the bench is
  a mess inside ten minutes, and that mess is the entire distinction this card
  draws. Nothing was ready. Everything was to hand, which is a much lower bar
  and the only one that has ever mattered. The first comfortable outcome is
  preparation becoming the project, with the bench arranged, photographed, and
  three months older than the work. Almost everybody does this, and you have a
  Sunday version of it. The second is acquisition. A fourth tool arrives to
  serve a job the first three were already too many for, because buying feels
  adjacent to progress, and the bench is fuller afterwards, and the afternoon
  is exactly the length it was. Neither failure is laziness, which is what
  makes both of them so durable.

The High Priestess   [M02]
keywords  the shower answer, second opinions, the dark half
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

UPRIGHT

 1. You answered it in the shower on Tuesday. Since then you asked four people.
  None of them told you anything you did not have at seven that morning. A
  crescent on the plate, half of it dark.
    Q. What did you already answer?

 2. Second opinions have a function and it is rarely information. A crescent
  moon, one edge lit. What you collect after deciding is permission. It gets
  gathered from people who cannot know the details. The shower had all of
  them.
    Q. Whose permission are you collecting?

 3. A crescent, half lit, half not, and the dark half is not empty. You know
  something you have not written down. It has been steady for a fortnight.
  Unwritten knowledge counts. It does not come with a receipt.
    Q. Where were you when you knew?

REVERSED

 1. You stopped asking on Thursday and went with the shower answer. It was
  right, or at least it was yours. Those two are closer together than the
  fourth opinion suggested. The fifth opinion was never collected. One
  decision, no committee.
    Q. When did you stop asking?

 2. You have four opinions and a decision you made before any of them arrived.
  Consultation after the fact is not research and never was. It is a search
  for one person to agree. It runs until one agrees.
    Q. Which one agreed with you?

 3. The dark half has gone flat and you cannot hear anything from it. That
  happens. It is usually noise rather than absence. A crescent with no lit
  edge is still a moon. Nothing has to be known this week.
    Q. What is the noise?

LIBRARY ENTRY

  The answer arrived in the shower on Tuesday morning at about seven.
  Everything since then has been consultation, four people deep, and not one
  of those conversations added a fact that was unavailable in the bathroom.
  This is not a criticism of asking. It is a description of what asking after
  the fact is for, which is permission rather than information, and permission
  has to be sought from people who by definition cannot know the details of
  your life. A crescent shows one lit edge, and there is something in the
  unlit part. Knowledge that has never been written down still counts, and it
  comes with nothing you can show anybody, which is why it feels weaker than
  the fourth opinion and generally is not. The search for agreement has a
  known ending. It stops when one person agrees. The fifth conversation is
  available and would add nothing.

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  Three states, and the first is the one worth having. Asking stops, the
  shower answer gets used, and it turns out to be right, or at least to be
  yours, and being right and being yours are nearer neighbours than the fourth
  opinion allowed. No committee was convened. The second state is consultation
  running after a decision that was already made, which has never been
  research of any kind, and which continues until one person agrees, at which
  point it ends abruptly and gets called consensus. The third is the flat
  spell. The dark half stops speaking, nothing surfaces, and a silent stretch
  is far more often static than emptiness, and an unlit crescent is still a
  moon on the plate. Nothing needs to be known this week. That is a
  permission. It is also the only week in three that has asked nothing of you.

  Pretending otherwise gets expensive in ways that take a while to show. The
  third is the ninth bowl. Nine went out, eight were delivered, and the cook
  ate standing at the counter, because care that only ever points outward is a
  habit and not a virtue. The ninth bowl exists. Your name is on it. Whoever
  counts the bowls is the person who cooked.

 3. You are running a Sunday that suits none of the people at the table.
  Inherited forms have an expiry. They do not announce it. Four people, one

    Q. Whose Sunday is it now?

  Sunday runs on the pattern of a house you have not lived in for twenty
  years. A roast at a fixed hour, a fixed order of events, a disagreement
  mid-afternoon. None of it was chosen, and the striking thing is how little
  that matters, because inherited practice does most of the work belief does

  centuries and functions without anybody holding a theory of bees. Where this

  here is narrow. Not whether the form is justified, but whether it still fits

LIBRARY ENTRY, REVERSED

  different house. That is a fact about that house and not about the people in
