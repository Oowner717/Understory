## Description — version A: lead with privacy

**Everything you write stays on your phone. No account, no cloud, nothing leaves the device.**

Understory is a tarot journal for people who want two quiet minutes, not a subscription to the cosmos. Draw one card a day from a deck of 78 botanical engravings — a naturalist's tarot where Swords bloom as gladiolus and Pentacles are the coin-pods of the honesty plant. Read a short reflection. Answer one question. Done before the kettle boils.

The deck pays attention. Draw a card you've written about before, and Understory shows you what you said last time it came up. Patterns surface on their own — your most-drawn cards, the balance of the suits — assembled entirely from your own entries, entirely on your own device.

No predictions. No accounts. No analytics. Export your whole journal as plain text whenever you like, because it's yours.

## Description — version B: lead with no-subscription

**Buy it once. It's yours. There is no subscription hiding in here.**

Understory is a daily tarot journal with a simple shape: one card, one short reading, one question worth writing about. The deck is a naturalist's cabinet — 78 specimen plates in the style of 19th-century botanical engraving, with the suits recast as flowering plants and every meaning written in one calm, dry voice.

When a card returns, the app shows you what you wrote the last time you drew it. That small moment — your own words, kept and resurfaced — is the whole engine. Over weeks, threads appear: which cards keep coming back, where your months lean.

Your journal never leaves your phone. Export it as plain text any time. Reflection, not fortune.

## Description — version C: lead with the deck

**A naturalist's tarot: 78 cards drawn as botanical specimen plates.**

Hawthorn for Wands. Bellflower for Cups. Gladiolus — the sword lily — for Swords, and Lunaria, the coin-podded honesty plant, for Pentacles. Understory's deck looks like it was lifted from a working naturalist's cabinet, and it's wrapped around the quietest journal you've ever kept.

The ritual is two minutes: draw the day's card, read a few plain sentences, answer one question. The app notices what you'd miss — when a card last appeared, what you wrote then, which suits your month leans on. It never predicts anything. It just keeps your attention where you left it.

No account. No analytics. Nothing leaves your device, and the whole journal exports as plain text.
